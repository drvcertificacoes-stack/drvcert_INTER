ISO 37301: Compliance Management System

Structures how legal, regulatory and voluntary obligations are met and builds a compliance culture across the organization.

What is ISO 37301

ISO 37301 is the international standard that sets out the requirements for a compliance management system. It helps organizations identify everything they must comply with, whether laws, regulations, contracts or voluntary commitments, and turn these obligations into processes, responsibilities and controls that work day to day.

The standard replaced an earlier compliance guidance document that was not certifiable. With it, compliance gained auditable requirements, and ISO 37301 certification became a recognized way of demonstrating that an organization’s compliance program is structured, effective and closely overseen by leadership.

Which organizations is it for

ISO 37301 can be adopted by companies, public bodies and nonprofit organizations of any size. It is especially sought after by:

  • regulated industries, such as financial services, healthcare, energy, telecommunications and utilities;
  • companies that contract with the public sector;
  • business groups that need to standardize compliance across several companies and countries;
  • organizations that want to build or strengthen their integrity program;
  • suppliers assessed by large customers in due diligence processes.

Key benefits

  • A complete view of obligations: the organization no longer depends on the memory of a few people and keeps an up-to-date register of what it must comply with.
  • Prioritized compliance risks: efforts focus where noncompliance would be most likely or have the greatest consequences.
  • A culture of integrity: expected behaviors are communicated, trained and reinforced by leadership.
  • Early detection of problems: reporting channels and monitoring allow action before failures turn into sanctions.
  • Credibility with regulators, customers and investors: certification shows that the program has been independently assessed.

Structure and main requirements

ISO 37301 follows the structure shared by management system standards. Its distinctive elements include:

  1. Compliance obligations: identify mandatory requirements and voluntary commitments, assess their impact on activities and keep the register up to date as things change.
  2. Compliance risk assessment: analyze where and how obligations could be breached and define proportionate controls.
  3. Governance and leadership: the governing body and top management set the compliance policy, provide resources and show commitment to the system.
  4. Compliance function: a person or team with the competence, authority, independence and direct access to leadership coordinates the system, without removing managers’ responsibility for compliance in their areas.
  5. Compliance culture: promote, at all levels, behaviors consistent with obligations, including compliance criteria in people management.
  6. Communication, training and awareness: make sure each person knows the obligations related to their role.
  7. Raising concerns and investigations: provide channels for reporting suspected violations, protect those who report from retaliation and investigate cases.
  8. Monitoring, audit and improvement: measure the performance of the system, address nonconformities and fix weaknesses at the root cause.

How the certification process works

  1. Gap analysis: the organization compares its existing compliance program with the requirements of the standard and identifies gaps.
  2. Implementation: the register of obligations, risk assessment, compliance function, training, reporting channels and indicators are put in place, followed by an internal audit and management review.
  3. Stage 1 audit: the certification body reviews the documentation, scope, risk assessment and governance structure.
  4. Stage 2 audit: auditors check, by sampling, how obligations are tracked, how reports are handled and how leadership and business units take part in the system.
  5. Certification and surveillance: once nonconformities are addressed, the certificate is issued, and periodic audits follow the system until recertification.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between ISO 37301 and ISO 37001?

ISO 37001 deals specifically with preventing bribery. ISO 37301 covers all of an organization’s compliance obligations. The two share the same structure and can be certified together.

Does ISO 37301 certification guarantee that a company complies with every law?

No. It shows that there is an effective system for identifying, monitoring and addressing obligations. No certification replaces legal analysis or completely eliminates the risk of noncompliance.

Who is responsible for compliance in the organization?

Every level. The compliance function coordinates and oversees the system, but each manager is accountable for meeting the obligations in their area, and leadership is accountable for the system as a whole.

Can the standard be applied to mid-sized companies?

Yes. Requirements are proportionate to the size, complexity and risks of the organization. In smaller companies, the compliance function can be held by one person, as long as they have adequate authority and independence.
